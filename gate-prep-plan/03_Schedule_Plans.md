# Phase-wise, Month-wise, Week-wise & Day-wise Plans (Sections F, G, H, I)

**Planning assumptions (stated, not guessed):**

- Target: GATE 2027 (IIT Madras), exams **6–7, 13–14, 20–21 Feb 2027**. CS + DA is an allowed two-paper combination — **register by 12 Oct 2026 (extended window, late fee)**.
- Time available: ~18 weeks (13 Oct 2026 → first exam ~6 Feb 2027).
- Capacity: 6–8 h/day ⇒ ~45–52 h/week; plan is built at ~48 h/week average with deliberate slack.
- If you are actually targeting **GATE 2028**: keep the same phase structure and multiply Phase 1–2 lengths ×2.5 and Phase 3–5 ×1.5 — the order and priorities stay identical.

**Why the plan is ordered this way (PYQ-driven):**

- Math (P&S/LA/DM) is the largest shared block on both papers (~13 m CS, ~27–30 m DA) and is pure skill — it goes **first** and stays warm all 18 weeks.
- Programming/DS/Algo + DBMS are shared and stable — Phase 1.
- CSE-only and DA-only branches are interleaved in Phase 2 so both papers always stay "live" (nothing is left untouched for months).
- PYQs are not a phase — they start **from week 1** (topic-wise, right after each topic) and get dedicated phases at the end.
- Non-MCQ (NAT/MSQ) is 35–50% of papers ⇒ every practice session mixes NAT-style exact-answer solving.

# I. Day-wise Study Timetable (templates, not 126 rigid days)

Daily templates are the realistic way to run a 4-month plan; every day is one of four types. The week grid above decides **which subjects** each day's blocks cover.

## I.1 Normal weekday (7 h)

| Slot | Duration | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| Block 1 (best-energy, morning) | 2.5 h | New theory of the day's main subject → immediately 8–12 solved examples |
| Block 2 (afternoon) | 2.5 h | PYQs of that topic (last 10 yrs, topic-wise) + notes into formula book |
| Block 3 (evening) | 1 h | Second subject (the parallel-track subject: DA topic or CSE topic) |
| Block 4 | 0.5 h | Spaced revision: yesterday's mistakes (D+1) + last-week flashcards (D+7) |
| Block 5 | 0.5 h | GA practice (alternate quant/verbal/analytical) or NAT-style drill |
| Rule | — | If Block 2 overruns, it eats Block 3 — PYQs outrank extra theory |

## I.2 Weekend day (8 h)

| Slot | Duration | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 h | Sectional/full test (timed, exam interface) |
| 2 | 2 h | Test analysis: every wrong/skipped question into mistake log with error code (Section M) |
| 3 | 1.5 h | Weak-topic repair from the analysis |
| 4 | 1 h | Weekly revision: week's formula book + mistake log skim |
| 5 | 0.5 h | Next-week planning + backlog triage |

## I.3 Low-energy / bad day (3.5 h, no guilt)

1. 1 h — PYQs of your **strongest** subject (kept deliberately easy to stay in motion)
2. 1 h — flashcards + formula book + yesterday's mistakes
3. 1 h — one recorded lecture at 1.5× on an 🟡/🟢 topic (passive but useful)
4. 0.5 h — GA

Rule: a bad day is a success if the **streak of PYQs + revision never breaks**. Never schedule new hard theory on a bad day.

## I.4 Catch-up strategy (when behind)

- **Behind <1 week:** weekend day absorbs it (drop the sectional test once; keep the analysis slot).
- **Behind 1–2 weeks:** invoke the **deprioritization ladder** — pause 🟢 topics first, then 🟡 theory (do their PYQs only), never 🔴 topics. Move the affected sectional test, never the mock dates.
- **Behind >2 weeks:** cut Phase 2 parallelism from 2 subjects to 1.5 (reduce the evening block's second subject) and extend Phase 3 by one week borrowed from Phase 4.
- Hard rule: **mock dates and Phase 3 PYQ marathon never move.** Slippage is absorbed by lowering depth on 🟡/🟢, not by postponing tests.
